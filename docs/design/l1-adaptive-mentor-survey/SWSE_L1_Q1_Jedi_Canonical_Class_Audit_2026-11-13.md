| Mechanic | Printed rule |
| --- | --- |
| Starting hit points | 30 + Constitution modifier |
| Hit points after first | 1d10 + Constitution modifier per Jedi level |
| Base attack bonus | +1 at Jedi level 1, full progression |
| Class defense bonuses | +1 Fortitude, +1 Reflex, +1 Will at level 1 |
| Force Points | 5 + floor(character level/2) upon gaining a level in Jedi; prior unspent Force Points lost |
| Starting skills trained | 2 + Intelligence modifier |
| Starting weapon | Lightsaber provided by the character's Master, per class feature |

### Jedi class skills

Acrobatics (DEX); Endurance (CON); Initiative (DEX); Knowledge (INT, each specialty separately); Perception (WIS); Pilot (DEX); Use the Force (CHA).

Persuasion, Stealth, Mechanics, and Treat Injury are not default Jedi class skills. Other legal sources may expand access, but Q1 cannot assert baseline training.

### Immediate versus later choices

Automatic starting feats and starting lightsaber are capabilities, not evidence of a player's specialization preference. At creation, the selected first-level talent and discretionary skill and feat selections can express intent. Force Training is NOT an automatic starting feat; it is a general feat requiring Force Sensitivity and trained Use the Force. A Jedi may satisfy these at level 1 with legal selection order and acquire Force powers through the feat. The survey does not grant powers.

### Additional available Force talent trees

Because the class grants Force Sensitivity, a Jedi may use an eligible talent choice to select a general Force talent from Alter, Control, Sense, or Dark Side (subject to printed prerequisites and Dark Side Score rules). Eligible Force tradition trees may also be available. Force talents and class talents are separate categories.

### Jedi class bonus feats

The printed Jedi bonus feat list at even Jedi levels includes:

Acrobatic Strike; Cleave; Combat Reflexes; Dodge; Double Attack; Dual Weapon Mastery I, II, III; Great Cleave; Improved Charge; Improved Disarm; Martial Arts I, II, III; Melee Defense; Mobility; Quick Draw; Power Attack; Powerful Charge; Rapid Strike; Running Attack; Skill Focus; Skill Training; Strong in the Force; Triple Attack; Triple Crit; Weapon Finesse; Weapon Focus (lightsabers).

Important: The Jedi's generic general feat at 1st character level is a separate opportunity from this even-level class-bonus list. Force Training is not in the printed Jedi class-bonus list.

### Supplemental review: Jedi Academy Training Manual

The book adds new talents to existing Jedi class trees (e.g., Consular: Adversary Lore, Know Weakness; Guardian: Grenade Defense, Hold the Line; Sentinel: Master of the Great Hunt; Lightsaber Combat: Shoto Focus). These expand legal class directions when that source is allowed.

The same manual also introduces Jedi Artisan, Jedi Instructor, and Jedi Investigator trees under its Jedi Knight talents discussion. Do NOT presume those are selectable by a level-1 Jedi; inspect the governing prestige-class access rules if designing a long-term aspiration about those trees. Do not mix starting-Jedi eligibility with later Jedi Knight eligibility.

## Design findings for the L1 Q1 survey

1. Replace early identity labels (Jedi Guardian/Consular/Sentinel/Duelist/Watchman) with methods the player wants to train in; Q2 handles career calling.
2. Separate innate Force Sensitivity from a deliberately selected interest in active Force powers (Force Training prerequisite).
3. Do not count automatically granted Force Sensitivity/lightsaber proficiency as preference evidence; count deliberately selected talent/skills/feats as appropriate and avoid duplicate counting.
4. Make each answer a first-person intention, not an assertion of currently possessed ability.
5. Do not assume the Jedi is a member of the Jedi Order or light-aligned: the Core description expressly notes Sith acolytes and other Force traditions can use the Jedi class.
6. Do not automatically turn a negotiation preference into a trained Persuasion skill; it is not a default Jedi class skill.
7. Make Q1 options reflect meaningfully different methods: blade, active Force power use, resilience/protection, sensing/investigation, influence/advising, and movement/initiative. These are candidate groupings only; support/discipline and Force-Control distinctions can be refined before approval.
8. The proposed Q1 must not lock future talent-tree selections or hide legal build options. It is an intent declaration.

## Open questions

- Is the player's Q1 about their single most important initial development focus, or a larger priority to guide the first several levels?
- Should ally-support and physical defense be one or separate first-level intentions? Their mechanical sources differ.
- Should agility/initiative be a standalone option, or be expressed through the Jedi Guardian and lightsaber approaches?
- Should explicit Force sensing versus active Force powers be separate selectable focuses?
- Do we want a neutral 'something different' answer to avoid forcing a narrow Jedi stereotype?

No question text or weights are considered approved by this audit.
